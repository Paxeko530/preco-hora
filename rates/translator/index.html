Typical costs for a translator

  • Computer-assisted translation tools and translation memories
  • Dictionaries and specialist terminology databases
  • Professional association fees and certifications
  • Computer, monitor and an ergonomic keyboard
  • Accountant, plus insurance if you handle sensitive documents

Add up your real costs and enter the monthly total in the fixed costs field.

Where billable time goes missing

Much of the non-billable time is quote requests that never go ahead, handling awkward file formats and terminology research before you start.

How to set your price

The usual unit is a per-word rate. Work out how many words you translate per hour and check that the per-word price covers your minimum hourly rate.

  • Charge per source word, but set a minimum per job to cover preparation time.
  • Review your rate when the text is highly technical or has complex formatting.
  • Rush jobs justify a clear surcharge agreed before you accept.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert an hourly rate into a per-word rate?

Divide your minimum hourly rate by the number of words you translate in an hour, including review.

Should I charge less for repeated text?

You can discount repetitions detected by your tool, but keep a minimum per job.
